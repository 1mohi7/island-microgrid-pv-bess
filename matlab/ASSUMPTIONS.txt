TIMEZONE - THE MOST DANGEROUS LINE IN THE STUDY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The original brief instructs a +6 hour shift on NASA POWER data. DO NOT APPLY IT
TO THIS FILE. The supplied solar file is ALREADY in Bangladesh local time.

Evidence: annual-mean global horizontal irradiance peaks in the hour-11 bin;
daylight runs hours 5-18; true solar noon at 91.4 deg east under a 90 deg east
standard meridian falls at 11:54, i.e. inside the hour-11 bin. Applying a further
+6 h shift would move the modelled solar peak to 17:00 and invalidate every
downstream result in a way that is very hard to spot.

loadFeederInputs.m RAISES AN ERROR rather than warning if this check fails.

3. OUTAGE MODEL
================================================================================
Fitted to the ONE supplied historical trace. Statistics of that trace:

  grid unavailability               758 h/yr (8.65%)
  shedding                          619 events, 671 h, mean 1.08 h, MAX 2 h
  fault                             11 events, 73 h, mean 6.6 h, max 13 h
  maintenance                       2 events, 14 h, 7 h each, daytime
  longest continuous outage         13 h

TWO CORRECTIONS TO THE BRIEF:

(a) THERE ARE FOUR OUTAGE CAUSES, not three. The trace contains
    'maintenance' as well as none/shedding/fault. It behaves as a hybrid:
    announced in advance like shedding, but 7 hours long like a fault.
    [DECISION] treated as FORESEEABLE for the reserve policy, fault-length for
    energy sizing, refill permitted beforehand.

(b) SHEDDING IS 1-2 HOUR BLOCKS, NOT SIX-HOUR DAILY BLOCKS. The brief predicts
    "diesel will likely dominate... six hours of daily shedding is a lot of
    energy for batteries to cover". The actual trace is 619 events averaging
    1.08 h. A two-hour critical-bus island at ~220 kW is about 440 kWh, well
    within a modest battery. Diesel is sized by the 13-hour fault and the
    72-hour stress case, NOT by shedding. This inverts the expected result.

SAMPLER STRUCTURE
  shedding      inhomogeneous Poisson, separable month x hour-of-day intensity
                (marginal-product form), empirical duration distribution
  fault         homogeneous Poisson, empirical duration
                [DECISION] CONSTANT arrival rate, not seasonal. Eleven observed
                events cannot support twelve monthly rates without fitting this
                particular year's pre-monsoon cluster as though it were structure.
  maintenance   homogeneous Poisson, daytime start, fixed 7 h duration

NON-OVERLAP PLACEMENT AND CALIBRATION [DECISION]
  Same-cause events are placed without overlapping OR abutting. Without that
  rule, two independently sampled one-hour shedding blocks landing in
  consecutive hours merge into an indistinguishable two-hour block; since the
  historical durations were themselves measured as contiguous runs, the
  inflation compounds on every resample and sampled traces end up containing
  six-hour shedding events that exist nowhere in the data.
  Rejection thins the arrival process, corrected by intensity multipliers solved
  by fixed-point moment matching at fit time (~1.16 shedding, ~1.08 fault).

  Verification, 200 sampled years vs the historical one:
    unavailability   8.64%  vs  8.65%
    shedding hours     667  vs    671
    fault hours         75  vs     73
    mean shedding duration 1.08 h vs 1.08 h
    worst continuous outage reaches 18 h vs the single year's 13 h - the sampler
    explores tail events the one historical year happened to miss, which is
    exactly what the held-out evaluation needs to be worth running.

CAUSE PRIORITY on overlap: fault > maintenance > shedding. Under-reporting
shedding this way is conservative: it stops the forewarning variant claiming
credit for hours no roster foresaw.

TRACE SETS
  in-sample    5 traces, seed 20250101, used in fitness
  held-out     100 traces, seed 77777777, SEALED - never used in fitness
  counterfactual  all-hours-available, used ONLY by the outage-blind design

reduction in the load profile, not as a supply-side decision variable.

  DEFINITION: HOURS-BASED - hours in which any critical load went unserved,

CONSTRAINT PENALTY [DECISION - CHANGED FROM THE BRIEF]
  penalty = 2.0e9 * violation + 5.0e10 * violation^2
  The brief specified a PURE QUADRATIC. Tested at realistic magnitudes it is far
  too weak exactly where it matters: at a weight of 5e8, a solution violating
  the threshold by 0.001 - one hour in a thousand - picks up a penalty of $500
  against an $8M objective and would come back as the winner while being
  infeasible. The quadratic only bites on gross violations, which the optimizer
  would have discarded anyway. The linear term makes that same violation cost
  $2M, exceeding the entire spread of net present cost across the feasible
  space. The winner's feasibility is VERIFIED and printed in every run.

CATALOGUE SNAPPING happens inside fitness evaluation ONLY. The wolf's stored
position stays continuous so the search landscape is not a staircase.

CACHING [DECISION - TWO BUG FIXES WORTH READING]
  (a) The cache key rounds to 1 kW AND THE EVALUATED SIZING IS QUANTISED TO THE
  SAME GRID. Those two must go together. An earlier version rounded only the key
  while leaving the sizing continuous, so 432.2 kW and 432.4 kW shared a cache
  entry while being different systems, and whichever was evaluated first had its
  result silently returned for the other. THE BUG IS INVISIBLE IN AGGREGATE -
  the optimizer still converges and every number still looks plausible - which
  is precisely what makes it dangerous. Quantising makes the cache LOSSLESS: one
  key, one system, one answer.

  (b) THE EVALUATOR (AND HENCE ITS CACHE) MUST NEVER BE SAVED. It is threaded
  through the optimizer to let later seeds reuse earlier seeds' work, and it
  grows by one entry per fitness call across all 100 iterations x 30 population
  x 12 seeds - many thousands of entries by the end of a run. greyWolfOptimizer.m
  returns it as a SECOND, separate output for exactly this reason: an earlier
  version attached it as result.evaluator, which meant every one of the 24
  saved per-seed run structs carried the entire accumulated cache, and -v7.3
  (HDF5) has real per-entry overhead for a containers.Map holding heterogeneous
  structs. That turned a results file that should be a few megabytes into
  roughly 900 MB. Thread the evaluator through a LOCAL variable between calls;
  never assign it into anything you intend to save.

11. DISPATCH RULES
================================================================================
Rule-based two-mode state machine. Deterministic, no solver.

GRID-CONNECTED: photovoltaic serves load -> surplus charges battery -> exports
subject to cap -> curtail; deficit met by battery down to reserveStateOfCharge(t)
-> grid import -> STEP 7 -> generator never runs.

*** STEP 7 IS AN ADDITION TO THE BRIEF AND IT IS NOT COSMETIC. ***
  If the state of charge is BELOW reserveStateOfCharge(t) and the hour is
  off-peak, import enough to reach the reserve target.
  The brief's grid-connected rules contain NO path by which the battery is ever
  charged from the grid, so the only charging source is photovoltaic surplus. On
  this feeder that surplus is rare - mean load 985 kW against 227 kW mean output
  from a 1,500 kW array - so the battery completes about 68 equivalent full
  cycles a year and can almost never REACH a raised reserve target. That makes
  the outage-aware reserve policy close to unactuatable, and it contradicts the
  brief's own description of a battery that "arbitrages freely against the
  tariff", since without grid charging there is no arbitrage.
  With step 7 the aware policy's fuel saving on identical hardware rises from 9%
  to 24%. It is deliberately the MINIMAL version: charge only to the reserve
  target, only off-peak, never above it. Full tariff arbitrage is NOT enabled.

ISLANDED: disconnect non-critical -> photovoltaic serves critical bus -> battery
down to stateOfChargeIslandFloor -> generator (min loading, fuel limited) ->
shed further -> record unserved critical BY CAUSE -> surplus charges battery,
remainder curtailed.

  Reference holding: the generator takes the voltage/frequency reference if the
  battery reaches its island floor, so island serving capacity is inverter
  rating PLUS generator rating.
  Headroom both ways: with the generator at 30% minimum loading and critical
  load below that, the surplus charges the battery or is curtailed. Reported as
  islanded curtailment, not absorbed into a slack variable.

ENERGY BALANCE, audited every hour:
  photovoltaic + batteryDischarge + generator + gridImport
    = servedLoad + batteryCharge + gridExport + curtailed
  Curtailment is a SINGLE bucket carrying both photovoltaic spill and generator
  minimum-loading surplus; splitting them would leave the balance open.
  The reference implementation closes this to 1e-13 kW in the worst hour.

WHY RULE-BASED, NOT MODEL-PREDICTIVE CONTROL [DECISION]
  Rule-based is what actually gets deployed, is transparent to a reviewer, and
  needs no solver in the loop. MPC would improve results but adds a forecast
  model, a solver and large run-time cost, and would let a reviewer argue the
  gains came from FORESIGHT rather than from SIZING. Named as future work.

12. THE TWO DESIGNS
================================================================================
  OUTAGE-BLIND   fitness on the all-hours-available counterfactual
                 reserve = stateOfChargeMinimum, constraint inactive
                 Represents conventional sizing that ignores grid unreliability.

  OUTAGE-AWARE   fitness on the five in-sample outage traces
                 reserve(t) from FITTED STATISTICS, constraint active
                 Represents outage-aware sizing AND operation.

  Both are re-scored on IDENTICAL traces. Crossing each design's SIZING with each
  design's DISPATCH POLICY gives four cells from only two optimizer runs, which
  separates the sizing effect from the operating effect.

*** NEVER REPORT THE BLIND DESIGN'S COST FROM ITS OWN OUTAGE-FREE RUN. ***
  Counter-intuitively it comes out HIGHER, not lower. On an all-available trace
  the feeder is billed for every kilowatt hour, whereas an outage trace
  disconnects non-critical load for 758 h/yr that nobody pays for. Outages look
  like a SAVING to a model that does not price unserved energy. Measured:
  $8.41M on its own trace vs $8.13M for the same sizing on outage traces.

CLAIRVOYANCE GUARD, ENFORCED STRUCTURALLY: the aware reserve policy reads
expectedForeseeableOutageHours, computed from fitted statistics conditioned on
month and hour of day. The statistics struct holds NO realised trace. It is not
possible for the aware controller to consult the trace it is about to be
simulated against, even by mistake. Only the FOREWARNED variant reads a trace,
and only its foreseeable causes - never faults.

13. DECLARED LIMITATIONS
================================================================================
1.  SINGLE-BUS ASSUMPTION. No internal network constraints, no voltage or
    thermal limits on feeder sections. At 1.5 MW this is a real simplification.
2.  MOTOR STARTING on critical water pumping is not modelled. Would require
    inverter overload capability and pump starting kVA.
3.  ONE WEATHER YEAR only. No weather sensitivity is possible with the supplied
    data.
4.  ONE HISTORICAL OUTAGE TRACE. All synthetic traces descend from it, so any
    structural feature it lacks is absent from the whole study.
5.  PHOTOVOLTAIC DEGRADATION is applied as an annual energy correction bought
    from the grid, not by re-dispatching each of the 25 years. Good here because
    the feeder imports in almost every hour; would not be in an islanded system.
6.  FAULT SEASONALITY not modelled - eleven events is too few to fit.
7.  LAND PERMITTING not modelled; only land PRICE is.
8.  72-HOUR STRESS CASE does not appear in the supplied trace (longest fault is
    13 h), so tank sizing must be tested as a separately injected scenario.
9.  Rooftop photovoltaic not modelled - see the note in section 9.

14. OPEN ACTION ITEMS, IN ORDER OF LEVERAGE
================================================================================
1.  LAND PRICE per acre near the feeder, and the acquisition premium multiple.
    Highest leverage of anything outstanding - swings optimal array 5x.
2.  LAND TENURE decision: purchase or lease. Matters nearly as much as price.
3.  LAND REAL APPRECIATION rate, or better, the SPREAD against the discount rate.
4.  FEEDER CUSTOMER-CLASS ENERGY BREAKDOWN, to replace the assumed 8.60 BDT/kWh
    import tariff with a derived one.
5.  GRID-FORMING INVERTER $/kW budgetary quote. Weakest capex line.
6.  INTERCONNECTION TRANSFORMER RATING, which sets the export cap.
7.  PHOTOVOLTAIC INSTALLED CAPEX from a local EPC.
8.  BATTERY CAPEX from a Bangladeshi importer of Chinese LFP.
9.  EXACT SUBSTATION COORDINATES for the NASA POWER pull.
10. DIESEL PRICE current at time of writing, cited as the BPC administered price.
